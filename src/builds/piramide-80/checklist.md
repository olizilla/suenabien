## Before cutting

- **Driver hole.** From Celestion's drawing:
  - The cut-out is 415 mm in the front and in the doubler ring. Test-fit the basket through the doubler, because the basket may flare just behind the flange. Open the hole to about 420 mm if it's tight.
  - The frame is 460 mm. That leaves 4.2 cm of flat wood to the nearest mouth corner, and 5.2 cm from the screw slots.
  - Behind the front, the doubler ring (49 cm across) clears the duct panels by 1.5 cm.
- **Fixing the driver.** It has 8 slots, 7 × 12 mm, on a 431–439 mm circle, every 45°. Sit the driver in place, mark through the slots, then take it out and drill pilot holes.
  - **Use screws,** into the 27 mm of front and doubler. That's BFM's advice: screws don't vibrate loose the way bolts can.
  - **On a prototype for trying other drivers,** use M6 bolts into tee nuts (or hurricane nuts) instead, so the driver can come in and out without wearing the holes. Other 18" drivers have their own cut-out and screw circle, so check before cutting.
- **Front set back 35 mm, no grille.** The driver's flange is 13 mm thick, and at its mechanical limit (Xmech 40 mm peak to peak) the cone can travel up to 20 mm forward. So 35 mm keeps it inside the box's front edges with 2 mm to spare. If a flat board or another box lands against the front, it can't reach the driver.
  - **Check it on the real driver:** lay a straightedge across the front edges of the box. The flange should be 22 mm or more behind it, and the cone and surround further still.
- **Depth.** The driver reaches 233 mm behind the front panel's face and is 160 mm across at the magnet. The front ring brace (25 cm from the box's front) runs round the walls, well clear of the basket.
- **Mirrored pairs.** Build two boxes with ducts top-left and bottom-right, and two with ducts top-right and bottom-left. The 2 × 2 stack then meets as a stepped diamond without turning any box, and the handles stay on the sides.
- **Handles.** Fit recessed cups in the sides, between the ring braces: about 28–48 cm back, centred on the height. That centres them on the weight, about 38 cm back. A cup up to 13 cm tall clears the duct panels by about 1.2 cm at the wall, so check it against your actual cups.
  - **Use sealed cups,** and glue and seal them in. Some recessed handles aren't airtight, and a leak there whistles like a bad port. The cup's flange also puts back most of the stiffness the cut-out takes from the wall.
- **Grain direction.** Cut the four walls with the face grain running front to back, along the 85 cm. Between the ring braces each wall bends mostly across its short span, so that's the direction to stiffen. It's free, and it's how the panels come off the sheet anyway.

## The back, with a 15 mm shell

BFM's back sits inside the box on 20 mm cleats (strips of 12 mm ply round the opening), cut 2 mm under the opening so it drops in. We keep that, and the 15 mm walls help:
- **The cleats get a solid fixing.** Glue them, and screw them through from the outside of the 15 mm walls, or into them from inside.
- **The back can be screwed straight into the 15 mm wall edges as well.** In 12 mm walls that's marginal; 15 mm birch takes a pilot-drilled screw well.
- **On the prototype, keep the back removable:** screw it to the cleats over foam gasket tape. Then you can get inside to measure the tuning, change the wadding, or fit a different driver's wiring. On the other boxes, glue and screw it.
- **The Speakon plate goes on the inside of the back,** over the hole. Run a 1 m lead from it out through the driver hole, and connect the driver last (BFM's order).

The 15 mm shell also makes the walls stiffer (by about a quarter, in panel frequency) and adds about 4 kg. The box is 85 cm deep, rather than 80, to make up for the thicker walls and the set-back front: the air inside (about 220 L) and the tuning stay as before.

## Building

BFM's tips from his plan:
- **Square corner joints:** screw a 50 mm wide, 50 cm long scrap of ply to one panel as a jig, clamp the other panel to it, glue and fasten, then take the jig off before the glue grabs it.
- **Straight circular-saw cuts:** make a saw sled from two strips of 12 mm ply (about 20 cm and 5 cm wide), screwed together, and trim it with the saw so its edge is the cut line.
- **Tight fits:** cut the front panel and the braces to a tight fit, then sand until they tap into place. Glue both surfaces so the glue isn't all scraped off on the way in.
- **Fasten every joint** with glue and screws (piloted and countersunk) or 18-gauge brads.
- **Outside edges:** a 10 mm round-over.

Then:
- **Spines.** Cut a 12 mm × 6 cm slot in the middle of the top and bottom of each ring brace. Once the rings are in, slide each spine in from the back through both slots, and glue it to the panel and into the slots. (A halving joint can't go together inside the closed box.) Stop the spines about 5 mm short of the brace on the back panel, or put felt on their ends. On the prototype the back comes off, and the spines mustn't rattle against its brace.
- **Duct panels.** Glue both long edges along their full length. A gap leaks air alongside the port: it can whistle and moves the tuning.
- **Printed wedges.** Each mouth has four pockets behind its front panel, where the triangular duct is wider than the stepped hole: two behind the teeth and two where the end steps meet the walls. Fill each with a 3D-printed wedge (see the mouth detail). That's 8 per box: 4 tooth wedges and 4 end wedges, two of them mirrored.
  - **Shape:** each wedge carries the step straight back, then eases down to the duct panel over 12 cm on a smooth S-curve.
  - **Material:** PETG or ASA, not PLA, which softens at about 55 °C in a hot van. Use 4 or more walls and 30–40% infill, so it doesn't buzz.
  - **Fixing:** glue with polyurethane construction adhesive or epoxy (PVA doesn't grip PETG). Fit the wedges before the front goes in, using the dry-fitted front as the stop. Allow about 1 mm of gap for the adhesive.
- **Round-overs:**
  - The tooth tips stay square: that's the Pirámide look.
  - Give the mouths' front edges a light 6 mm round-over, including the wall ends inside each mouth. The outline still reads as crisp steps.
  - Round both faces of the duct panels' inner (back) end edge, about 6 mm.
- **Seal everything.** Glue every joint, and fit a gasket on the driver and on the Speakon plate. Leaks chuff and whistle like a bad port.
- **Wadding.** Fit 5 cm on the two quarters of the back away from the duct ends, clear of the cleats and the back brace. Keep it at least 10 cm from the duct openings.

## Testing the prototype

Our model only estimates each wall's lowest resonance from its size and its braces. So test the first box before building the rest:
- **Knock test:** tap each panel. A dull thud is good; a ring means it needs more bracing.
- **Sweep test:** play a slow sine sweep from 20 to 200 Hz at a moderate level, with a hand on each panel in turn, and feel for buzzing.
- **If a panel buzzes,** add a brace there. If nothing does, the bracing is right, and that's also the test of whether one ring brace would be enough (it saves about 1.3 kg).

## Measuring and setting up

- **Tuning.** Check it on the first box with the back screwed on over its gasket.
  - With an impedance meter (DATS, or REW with a resistor), the dip between the two peaks is the tuning.
  - The low-tech way: play a quiet slow sine sweep and find where the cone barely moves while the ports blow hardest.
- **Don't trim to tune.** A centimetre of duct moves the tuning only about 0.13 Hz, and 6 cm moves it about 1 Hz. Anything from 23 to 27 Hz is fine. Set the DSP high-pass (LR4) to 0.8 × the measured tuning, which is about 20 Hz for 25 Hz.
- **Low-pass at 100 Hz or below.** The duct's pipe resonance and the box's front-to-back resonance sit near 200–220 Hz. An LR4 at 100 Hz takes them down by about 25–28 dB.
- **Amp: no limiter needed.** BFM limits his amp to 95 V, but the Q10 only has gain controls, and that's fine here. In our model, with the 20 Hz high-pass, the TSQ1845 only reaches its rated excursion (Xmax) at about 101 V, and the Q10 tops out at 98 V into 8 Ω. Its 1200 W is also under the driver's 1800 W rating. Two boxes in parallel on one channel (4 Ω, 95.4 V) have a little more margin and lose about 0.2 dB per box.

## Port noise (chuffing)

The air moves fastest in the stepped mouths. These are the model's air speeds with each box playing about 110 dB at 1 m. That's roughly four boxes giving 100 dB at the desk:

| Frequency | 25 Hz | 30 Hz | 35 Hz | 40 Hz |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Air speed in the mouth | 10 m/s | 6 m/s | 4 m/s | 2.5 m/s |

- **Rounded edges stay quiet up to about 17 m/s,** which this box reaches only at its 115 dB ceiling at 25 Hz.
- **Sharp edges can start to hiss from about 10 m/s.**

The noisy spots would be:
- the mouths' front edges, including the square tooth tips;
- the pockets behind the teeth, where air leaving the duct hits the back of the front panel;
- the duct's inner end;
- any leak.

The printed wedges deal with the pockets, and the round-overs and sealing deal with the rest. Square tooth tips with a 6 mm round-over on the face edges should be quiet at normal levels. Totally sharp edges would bring the onset of noise down by roughly 4–5 dB at 25 Hz, to about 110 dB per box, which is around a loud night's level.
